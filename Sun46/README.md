# Sun's Conjecture 4.6 on twin primes and strict partitions is false

Machine-checked in Lean 4: `Sun46.conjecture_false` in [`Final.lean`](Final.lean), depending
only on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. The counterexample is n = 267497.

## The conjecture

Let φ be Euler's totient function and let q(m) be the number of partitions of m into
distinct parts (the strict partition function, OEIS A000009).

Z.-W. Sun, *Problems on combinatorial properties of primes*, arXiv:1402.6641 (v12, 2016);
in *Number Theory: Plowing and Starring through High Wave Forms*, World Scientific, 2015,
pp. 169–187:

> **Conjecture 4.6** (2014-01-07). For any integer n ≥ 60, there is a positive integer
> k < n such that m ± 1 and q(m) + 1 are all prime, where m = φ(k) + φ(n − k)/4.

It is also part (i) of the conjecture in OEIS A235343, whose Mathematica program fixes the
reading: k runs over 1, …, n − 1 and all three of m − 1, m + 1, q(m) + 1 must be prime.
Remark 4.6 says the conjecture was verified for n up to 10^5. The introduction of the paper
says: "The author would like to offer 1000 Chinese dollars as the prize for the first
complete solution to any one of the 60 conjectures."

## The result

**For n = 267497 there is no such k.** 267497 is prime. Of the 267496 values of k:

| | number of k |
| --- | --- |
| φ(n − k) is not divisible by 4, so m is not an integer | 18149 |
| m is an integer, but m − 1 or m + 1 is composite | 241670 |
| m is an integer, m − 1 and m + 1 are prime, and q(m) + 1 is composite | 7677 |

The 7677 values of k in the last row give 1752 distinct values of m, between 17292 and
267228. For each of them q(m) + 1, a number of 100 to 403 digits, is composite: 1581 have a
prime factor below 10^5 and the other 171 fail Fermat's test to base 2.

## The Lean proof

The statement is in [`Statement.lean`](Statement.lean):

```lean
def q (m : ℕ) : ℕ := (Nat.Partition.distincts m).card

def Works (n k : ℕ) : Prop :=
  0 < k ∧ k < n ∧ ∃ m : ℕ, 4 * m = 4 * φ k + φ (n - k) ∧
    (m - 1).Prime ∧ (m + 1).Prime ∧ (q m + 1).Prime

def Conjecture : Prop := ∀ n : ℕ, 60 ≤ n → ∃ k, Works n k

theorem conjecture_false : ¬ Conjecture
```

`works_iff_rat` shows that `4 * m = 4 * φ k + φ (n - k)` with `m : ℕ` is the same as
`m = φ k + φ (n - k) / 4` over the rationals. The proof has three parts.

1. **Every k** (`Sweep.lean`, `Chunks/`). The kernel computes φ(k) and φ(n − k) for each k
   and tests m − 1 and m + 1 for primality with one `gcd` against the product of the primes
   below 521. `Arith.lean` proves these procedures correct for numbers below 521². The
   conclusion is that every m that could matter is one of 1752 listed numbers.
2. **The values of q** (`Recurrence.lean`, `Kronecker.lean`, `Pack.lean`, `Digits.lean`,
   `Bound.lean`, `Number.lean`, `Check1.lean` to `Check3.lean`, `Values.lean`). From
   Mathlib's pentagonal number theorem and its generating function for partitions into
   distinct parts, ∏(1 + xⁱ) · ∏(1 − xⁱ) = ∏(1 − x²ⁱ) gives a recurrence for q with about
   850 terms.
   Substituting x = 2^1504 turns it into one identity between three integers of 4·10^8
   bits. The kernel builds the table K = Σ q(a)·2^(1504·a) by a Newton iteration, checks the
   identity with two multiplications, and reads off 1752 of its digits. A separate bound,
   q(n) ≤ e^516·(517/516)^n < 2^1504 for n ≤ 267228, shows the digits do not overlap.
   The result is `q_table`: 1752 values of the strict partition function, proved from the
   definition.
3. **Compositeness** (`Composite.lean`). For each value the kernel checks a divisor of
   q(m) + 1 or runs Fermat's test.

`Data.lean`, `Table.lean` and `Chunks/` are generated by `scripts/gen_lean.py` and
`scripts/gen_table.py`. Nothing in them is trusted: the table values are re-derived in part 2.

Building it from nothing takes about nine minutes on four cores, with a peak of about 7.5 GB
of memory (measured: 504 seconds, 7.45 GB):

```bash
lake build
```

## Checks outside Lean

These are independent of the Lean proof and of each other, and they cover more than it does.
The programs are in [`scripts/`](../scripts).

- **Sun's own data** (`compare_oeis.py`). The counts computed here agree with Sun's table
  of A235343 in the OEIS, all 10000 terms, and with its three worked examples. The numbers m
  with m − 1, m + 1 and q(m) + 1 all prime agree with his table of A235344, all 51 terms,
  the last being 3235368. His announcement of the conjecture (Number Theory List,
  15 January 2014) gives the 49th such m as 2902518, with q(m) + 1 of 1337 digits; both
  agree.
- **Four computations of the counterexample**, sharing no code: Euler's pentagonal
  recurrence with GMP (`witness_sets.py`, `count_witnesses.py`); a brute-force loop with
  SymPy and Gauss's identity q(m) = Σ p((m − t)/2) over triangular numbers t
  (`verify46.py`); FLINT's partition function (`extend46.py`); PARI/GP (`verify46.gp`).
- **The range around it** (`extend46.py`). n = 267497 is the only n with 60 ≤ n ≤ 10^7
  that has no suitable k. 27 other values of n in that range have exactly one, the largest
  being 403342. "No suitable k" at 267497 is rigorous. "Some suitable k" elsewhere relies
  on probable-prime tests for the numbers q(m) + 1 with m > 10^6; those with m below
  267497 have been proved prime with PARI, so 267497 is the smallest counterexample.
- **The companion conjecture** (`extend46.py N 3 ii`). Sun announced a second part with
  the first: for n > 1234 some k has m = φ(k) + φ(n − k)/8 with m − 1, m + 1 and q(m) − 1
  all prime. It has no failure for n ≤ 10^6 (probable-prime tests), and the m found agree
  with A235346. It is not in the paper and nothing here is about it.

## Why it fails

Let T be the set of m with m − 1, m + 1 and q(m) + 1 all prime (A235344). Since
log q(m) ~ π·sqrt(m/3), the number q(m) + 1 is prime with probability of order 1/sqrt(m),
which together with the twin prime condition makes T very sparse: 36 elements up to 10^6
and 68 up to 10^7. There is none between 44700 and 134922, just beyond the range Sun
checked. For n = 267497 the integer values of m lie between 14422 and 267482; T has nine
elements there (15138, 17388, 21600, 40038, 44700, 134922, 156258, 187908, 243708) and
none is of the form φ(k) + φ(267497 − k)/4. The neighbours are unremarkable: n = 267496
has 19 suitable k and n = 267498 has 30.

## Status

- **Proved in Lean** that the conjecture is false. Not proved in Lean: that 267497 is the
  smallest or the only counterexample below 10^7. Those are computations.
- **Not reviewed** by a person.
- **Novelty.** Checked on 22 November 2026, and none mentions this counterexample:
  - the live OEIS entries A235343 and A235344 (last edited 5 November 2025), which still
    state the conjecture as open;
  - the paper's arXiv version, Sun's 2014 talk slides of the same title, and his
    announcement on the Number Theory List, which all state the conjecture for n ≥ 60;
  - Sun's homepage (last modified 4 August 2026), which lists the paper as "My 60 Open
    Problems on Combinatorial Properties of Primes" and says nothing about this conjecture;
  - the six works that OpenAlex lists as citing the paper, none of which is about partition
    functions, judging by their titles;
  - the OEIS Open project (arXiv:2608.11941; results repository as of 5 November 2026),
    whose 492 conjectures do not include A235343 or A235344;
  - a MathOverflow search for "strict partition" twin primes Sun (no results), and web
    searches.

  Not checked: replies to the announcement on the Number Theory List, Sun's 2021 book of
  conjectures, and Chinese-language sources.
- **Precedent.** Sun's "2-4-6-8 conjecture" (A306477) was disproved in 2026 by the OEIS Open
  project. The OEIS entry records this in a comment of 20 October 2026 with a link to a
  Lean proof. For that conjecture Sun had offered a prize for the first explicit
  counterexample as well as for a proof.
- **Related work.** `F8Labs-ai/sun_conjectures` on GitHub (dated early November 2026)
  proves Conjectures 2.7(i) and 2.14(ii) of the same paper in Lean, and disproves three
  conjectures of another paper of Sun. It does not mention Conjecture 4.6.
- **The prize** is Sun's to award. The paper offers it for "the first complete solution";
  whether a disproof counts, and whether the 2014 offer stands, is his decision. His
  address in the paper is zwsun@nju.edu.cn.
